## Start simple

The fastest way to make a bad gabber kick is to start too complicated.

Begin with a clean transient, a body that already moves in the direction you want, and a distortion stage that has one job. If every stage is trying to do everything, the result usually gets wider, dirtier, and less controlled without actually sounding harder.

## Listen for movement, not only damage

The kick still needs shape. When the low and mid information stop moving, the distortion can feel impressive for a few seconds and then become tiring immediately.

Things I usually check first:

- Whether the click still reads clearly after the first distortion stage.
- Whether the low end is staying stable or getting blurry.
- Whether the tail has motion instead of turning into a flat wall.

## Push in stages

Instead of trying to get the whole character from one plugin, split the work:

1. Use the first stage to define the body.
2. Use EQ to remove the most obvious mud.
3. Use the next stage to add aggression where the kick can actually carry it.

That gives you better control when you later decide whether the kick should lean more classic gabber, more hardcore, or eventually more uptempo.
